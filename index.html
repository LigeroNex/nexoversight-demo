The problem

Who is in charge of this agent, and how do you know?

An AI agent in production can pay, refund, change a customer record or write to someone outside the organisation. Someone is accountable for what it does. Sooner or later a board, an auditor, a regulator or a customer asks that question, and the answer is rarely on record.

Approvals leave no record

Which actions did a person approve, on what basis, and what were they shown? Approvals happen in chat messages and inboxes, if at all.

Nobody can say what it saw

When something goes wrong, the trail sits in an engineer's tracing tool: incomplete, technical and hard to put in front of a committee.

Actions are hard to attribute

Which user asked, which agent acted, and which tool did it? The answer is spread across systems that were never joined up.

The stop is assumed

Everyone believes the agent can be stopped. Few can say when that was last rehearsed, how long it took, or what ran afterwards.

How it works

Your systems enforce. NexOversight decides who may authorise, and records it.

NexOversight sits beside your agents, not in their path. For the actions you choose to route, your orchestrator holds the action until it has a valid decision. NexOversight puts the request before a named person, signs the decision and keeps the record.

  1. 1Your agent reaches a consequential action
  2. 2Your orchestrator pauses it and asks
  3. 3A named person decides before the deadline
  4. 4A signed decision comes back, valid once
  5. 5Your orchestrator acts, or not: no decision, no action
  6. 6What ran is checked against what was approved
  1. 01

    Approve

    Did a person with authority approve what mattered?

    Thresholds you set, written as rules you can read, route consequential actions to an approver role. The deadline comes from your policy and fails closed: no decision in time means no action.

  2. 02

    Bind

    Could an approval be used for something else?

    Each decision is signed, bound to the exact tool and arguments, and valid once, for a short time. The bridge in your orchestrator refuses anything else.

  3. 03

    Replay

    What did the agent see, decide and do?

    A timeline rendered from the record, never by re-running the agent. Each step says whether its content was captured, redacted, truncated or absent, and gaps are shown as gaps.

  4. 04

    Reconcile

    Did anything run without approval?

    Executed actions are checked against approvals. An action with no matching approval, or a different one, becomes a finding for a person to triage.

  5. 05

    Stop

    Can we stop it, and how would we know?

    Hold its requests and signal your orchestrator's stop endpoint. Revoking access and isolating it stay your runbook steps, prompted and timed. A stop counts as verified only on acknowledgement and an empty record after it.

  6. 06

    Record

    Can we show this to someone entitled to doubt us?

    A hash-chained record anyone can verify, and a report of the conditions of human involvement: who decided, what they were shown and how long they took, as counts with their denominators.

Content stays out by default. NexOversight records structure: who asked, which agent acted, which tool it used, the outcome and a digest of the arguments. Message content and tool arguments are captured only where you turn them on, agent by agent and field by field.

Limits

What NexOversight never does.

An oversight product is only worth having if it is honest about where its reach ends. These are rules, not settings, and they are put in writing at the start of every engagement.

  • No scoreNothing is reduced to one number, grade or percentage. You get counts with their denominators, with what was not observed shown beside what was.
  • No claimed stopsIt holds requests and signals your stop endpoint. It reports a stop as verified only when the acknowledgement and the record show it, and never says it stopped an agent.
  • No credentials heldIt holds no credential to any of your systems and cannot act on them. Revoking an agent's access and isolating it stay your steps.
  • Not in the agent's pathNo proxy, guardrail or filter. It never inspects, blocks or rewrites what an agent does; your orchestrator enforces the decision.
  • Never re-runs an agentReplay renders what was recorded. Re-running a model or a tool shows what it might do now, and could repeat a payment.
  • No model decidesThresholds are rules, decisions are made by people, and reconciliation is a set comparison. No AI sits on any approval, stop or evidence path.

Rules for every approval, stop and report

  • Fails closedNo valid decision before the deadline means no action. A request that times out is recorded separately from one that was rejected.
  • Content off by defaultStructure, not content. A field is captured only where you turn it on, and every view of captured content is recorded with its purpose.
  • Relative to what you sendReconciliation sees only the telemetry you send, and every report says so. An action never reported can be found only in an independent record you supply.
  • Drills on harmless workStop drills run against a harmless task you define, with controls that can be undone, never against live work.
  • People are not rankedApprover patterns are reported by role. A view by named person needs a documented purpose, and the view itself is recorded.
  • A record, not a verdictThe report records the conditions of human involvement. Whether that involvement was meaningful is your judgement. NexOversight certifies nothing.
